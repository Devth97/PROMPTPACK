Recurring bills and financial organisation

Compare two invoices for a price change

Comparable line items matter more than a simple total difference.

Start with the task and the source

Organise redacted records and questions; keep decisions and account access under your control. Use the original record, approved brief or supplied specification as your starting point. The worked example below shows one way to organise the task; replace its assumptions only with information you have checked.

  1. Write down the input and the question you want to resolve.
  2. Separate confirmed facts from missing or ambiguous information.
  3. Follow the example with your own verified inputs, preserving units, scope and source references.
  4. Complete the review check before sending, publishing or acting on the result.
Fictional worked example

Work through the example

The starting point

A fictional January invoice charges two units at 20 each. February charges three units at the same unit rate. The total rises from 40 to 60, before any other stated charges.

What to do

Align quantity, period and unit rate before comparing totals. Here the rate is unchanged and the quantity increases by one. Record the 20 difference as a quantity-driven change under the supplied facts, then verify whether the additional unit was expected.

What the result means

This comparison does not prove overbilling or a price increase. Check the real service scope and invoice details before contacting the provider. Keep different billing periods or currencies out of a simple like-for-like total.

Check before you use it

Reconcile the line items with both original invoices.

Keep the original source available so you can compare it with the draft. Leave unresolved questions visible rather than filling them with guessed facts. An AI-generated draft is an aid to review; it does not perform a payment, send a message, publish content or establish a professional conclusion.

Optional prompt to organise your draft

Replace the bracketed placeholders with fictional or anonymised information. Do not paste credentials, payment details, client records or confidential family documents. Use the prompt to prepare a draft, then check it against the method above.

Compare these fictional invoices [A and B]. Align service period, quantity, unit rate, discount and other stated charges. Calculate differences only for comparable entries. Explain unknown or changed scope. Do not label a higher total an error without checking whether the service or period changed.
