UGC Ad Script Template: Hook, Demo and Shot List

A UGC ad script connects a specific viewer problem to a product demonstration and one next action. Plan what the viewer will see alongside what the presenter will say. Use supplied facts, identify paid or fictional staging, and check that the demonstration supports the claims in the script.

By PromptPack · Reviewed 28 October 2026

Copy the templateWorked exampleUse and review itChoose your next step

What do you need?

A product you can demonstrate, supplied facts, permission to use the media, a recording or generation tool and an editor. AI accounts and credits are separate.

Copy and customize this prompt

Replace each bracketed field. Keep unknown facts marked as missing so they can be resolved before production.

Plan a short product-demonstration ad for [PRODUCT] and [AUDIENCE].
One visible task: [WHAT THE PRODUCT DOES ON CAMERA].
Verified facts and sources: [FACTS]. Missing evidence: [UNCONFIRMED CLAIMS].
Presenter relationship to the product: [CUSTOMER, EMPLOYEE, PAID PRESENTER OR FICTIONAL CHARACTER].
Write six scene briefs: hook, context, setup, demonstration, observation and next action.
For each scene provide spoken words, visible action, framing, on-screen text and evidence needed.
Use [TARGET DURATION] as a rehearsal target, not a measured recording time.
Next action and real destination: [CTA].
Do not invent personal experience, testimonials, results, urgency or discounts.
Flag sponsorship disclosures, permissions and claims that need review before publication.

Worked example: inputs, outputs and revisions

Flags before publication
- Disclosure: the presenter works for PromptPack. Say so on screen and in the caption ("I work on PromptPack").
- Scene 1 "Here's what I use now" implies personal use; confirm it is true for this presenter or remove it.
- Scene 5 shows an AI response; outputs vary by tool and prompt edits. Do not imply a guaranteed result.
- Permissions: the text assistant's interface appears on screen; check its brand guidelines before running ads.
- No price, discount or urgency is stated; add the price only if it is current on the recording date.

What the review found

  • Every product fact in the script matched the brief: 29-page guide, 28 prompts and the worked example.
  • The hook “Here’s what I use now” claims personal use. The output flagged it for review, but the brief had asked for no invented personal experience.
  • The disclosure appeared in the flags but in no scene, so a viewer watching without the caption would miss it.
  • Scene 1 needed three mismatched slides that nobody had supplied, and scene 5 said what the assistant “gives” in general rather than what the recording shows.
  • The six spoken lines total 71 words: roughly 24 to 28 seconds at 2.5 to 3 words per second. That is an estimate for rehearsal, not a recording time.

Revision prompt

Revise scenes 1 and 5 only.
1. Replace the hook with one the video can show without personal claims or borrowed assets: open on the kit's worked-example slides. Put the disclosure "I work on PromptPack" in scene 1's on-screen text, not only in the caption.
2. In scene 5, describe only what is visible: the response the assistant returned on the recording day, with the tool name and date on screen. Do not say what it "gives" in general.
Keep the other scenes and the flags.

Revised scenes

Revised scenes (version 2)

1 Hook
Spoken: "Six slides that look like one set. This is the worked example inside Carousel Studio."
Visible: the kit's six worked-example slides shown one after another on a laptop.
Framing: screen recording, then presenter in a small corner frame.
On-screen text: "I work on PromptPack · Carousel Studio worked example"
Evidence needed: the slides are the kit's own example (edition 2.1).

5 Observation
Spoken: "This is the plan it returned today: one line for each of the six slides."
Visible: the assistant's actual response, unedited, scrolled slowly.
Framing: screen recording.
On-screen text: "[Assistant name] · [recording date] · results vary"
Evidence needed: keep the screen recording file; do not trim the response to look shorter.

Flags carried over
- Disclosure now appears in scene 1 and must also appear in the caption.
- Check the assistant's brand guidelines before showing its interface in a paid ad.
- Rehearse aloud and measure; 75 spoken words is an estimate of 25–30 seconds at 2.5–3 words per second, not a measurement.

The revised hook shows the kit’s own worked example, the disclosure is on screen in scene 1, and scene 5 describes only the response recorded on the day.

Limitations of this example

  • The ad was not recorded, edited or run, so there is no watch time, click or sales result to report.
  • A seller demonstrating their own product is not a customer testimonial and should not be presented as one.
  • The spoken length is an estimate. Rehearse aloud and time it.
  • Showing another company’s app in an ad can be subject to its brand rules; check before publishing.

How do you choose a hook you can demonstrate?

Start with an observable task, such as finding an appointment note or comparing two packing layouts. The opening should promise what the video can actually show. A product being visible in a scene does not substantiate claims about savings, health, revenue or typical customer outcomes.

Use the presenter's real relationship to the product. A paid presenter or generated character should not be scripted as a satisfied customer with an invented purchase history. Write the disclosure and review requirements into the brief before generating or recording the scenes.

How do you move from script to edit?

  1. Fill the brief with supplied facts and one visible task.
  2. Storyboard the product interaction before writing more dialogue.
  3. Rehearse aloud and measure the actual duration.
  4. Record or generate the scenes using a consistent product appearance and framing.
  5. Edit the demonstration, then add readable captions and the approved next action.

For generated scenes, inspect hands, labels, product shape and action continuity. A persuasive-looking image can still misrepresent how a product works. Replace or simplify a scene that cannot depict the task accurately.

What should you review before running the ad?

Read the script and on-screen text together. Check every claim against supplied evidence, review the presenter relationship and required disclosures, confirm media permissions, and test the destination. Watch the edit at phone size with sound off as well as on. Test one creative change at a time and judge it using your own results; the template does not predict performance.
