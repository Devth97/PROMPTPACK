2 / 6 Context
Headline: People who can't see your image still need what it adds
Body: Screen readers read the alt text aloud. Empty alt tells them to skip the image.
Image: a speech bubble coming out of an image frame.
Source: W3C WAI images tutorial. Gap: the decision tree page itself does not describe screen readers; cite the WAI Images Tutorial if this line stays.
Alt text: An image frame with a speech bubble reading its alt text aloud.

3 / 6 Explanation
Headline: Five jobs, five answers
Body:
Decoration → alt=""
Link or button → where it goes
Adds meaning → describe the meaning
Chart or diagram → explain it on the page
Text in the image → repeat the text
Image: five small icons in a column, one per row.
Source: W3C WAI, An alt Decision Tree (13 May 2024).
Alt text: A list of five image jobs and the alt text each one needs, as listed in the slide text.

4 / 6 Worked example (fictional)
Headline: One bakery homepage, three images
Body:
Logo linking home → alt="Rosa's Bakery home"
Photo of today's sourdough → alt="Round sourdough loaf with a dark, crackled crust"
Wavy divider line → alt=""
Label: Fictional example.
Image: a simple page wireframe with the three images marked 1, 2, 3.
Source: decision tree cases (functional, informative, decorative).
Alt text: A page sketch with a linked logo, a bread photo and a divider, each labelled with its alt text.

5 / 6 Practical check
Headline: Check one page in ten minutes
Body: List every image. Write its job next to it. Then write or fix the alt text. Ask: would someone miss anything if the image vanished?
Image: a checklist with three ticks.
Source: method based on the decision tree; the ten-minute figure is an estimate, not a measurement. Remove it or label it.
Alt text: A three-item checklist: list images, name each job, fix the alt text.

Image: the image frame from the cover, now with a tick.
Source: link to the decision tree in the caption.
